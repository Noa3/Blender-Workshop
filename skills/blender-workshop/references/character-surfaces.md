## Anime, cel shading and line work

### Choose the style before the shader
Start with two small style studies: neutral front light and a backlit three-quarter view. Note silhouette, shadow shape, line weight, palette and background contrast separately. Aim for a readable image rather than as many toon effects as possible. Skin, hair and clothing can have different shadow colors and thresholds. The character should still read in a thumbnail without lines.

### Recipe A: light-driven bands in EEVEE (rendered)
1. Select EEVEE as the actual render engine. Connect `Diffuse BSDF (white) → Shader to RGB: Color → Color Ramp: Fac`.[4] White gives a light response; apply the palette **afterward**. A colored diffuse input can shift the threshold under identical lighting depending on base color.
2. Set ramp interpolation to **Constant** and add three elements: shadow, midtone and light. Connect `Color → Emission (Strength 1) → Material Output: Surface`. Do not add another Diffuse afterward or it will relight the finished colors. The lab mask transitions at 0.26 and 0.56: starting points for **this** light setup, not universal shader settings.
3. Use one directional key; hold fill, world, exposure and transform fixed while matching. If only one band appears, view `Shader to RGB` without the ramp and move the thresholds into the actual brightness range. Give the background its own flat color.
4. Look for a connected light edge across forehead, nose and cheek. Do not approve only the front: turn the head, move the lamp separately and inspect a thumbnail. If bands shimmer, remove high-frequency normal detail or slightly widen the transition. Downsampling alone does not fix temporal instability.
5. **Limit:** `Shader to RGB` is EEVEE-specific, interrupts the physical shader path and does not give expected results in every pass.[4] The tested kitsune mask places eye and cheek shapes on the shell; it does **not** demonstrate Grease Pencil Line Art or a facial rig.

### Recipe B: other render targets
- **Cycles:** Test Toon BSDF (Diffuse/Glossy), using `Size` for angle and `Smooth` for the transition.[32] This is a different shader, not a drop-in adapter for Shader to RGB. Render a sphere under one light before the character. For an exact palette, test an art-directed emission approach independently of physical lighting.
- **Art-directed cross-engine shader:** Use a normalized surface normal and normalized direction in the **same coordinate system**. Connect `Vector Math: Dot Product → Map Range (−1…1 to 0…1) → Constant Ramp → Emission`. Check the sign with an unmistakably lit test face. This simulates a fixed light direction; without additional shadow calculations, a nose or cape will not cast real occlusion. Rebuild the shader in the game/web runtime or bake appropriate data; arbitrary Blender nodes are not a glTF material.[20]
- **Face and hair:** Model one broad forehead/cheek plane and a few hair locks first. If turning makes a stylized face unreadable, try a dedicated facial shadow mask rather than more realistic normal detail. For a painted direction/SDF mask, derive light side in local head space, mirror for left/right and blend the threshold gently while turning. This is an art-direction option: validate mirrored UVs, profile and the transition through front view on the actual character. Run highlight bands along clump growth; large graphic shapes come before individual strands.

### Stage a drawing in space (2.5D)
For an illustrated landscape, separate three groups in depth: foreground framing, middle plane for the subject and distant background. Read the composition from the target camera, then move it slightly. Does parallax add depth or open a gap? Sharon Snow's conference demo compares working and camera views at 14:24/14:30.[45] Resolve eyes, mouth and main facial shadow as a drawing first. Try dropping an outer line on the lit side where value and background already separate the form. This is a style choice, not a requirement for another outline shader or a particular brush.[45]

### Lines: three tools, three decisions
| Method | Implementation | Acceptance test |
|---|---|---|
| Designed/modelled lines | Make eyes, liner, brows and cheeks from tapered strips or curves fitted just above the carrier. Lab points follow the curved mask. | Inspect both sides: no floating or buried strokes and no Z-fighting. |
| Freestyle | Create a View Layer line set for render contours. Start with **Silhouette/External Contour**. Add folds/creases selectively; exclude with collections/face marks.[16][34] | Render at final resolution. Too many interior lines → restrict edge types instead of thinning every stroke. |
| Grease Pencil + Line Art | Create a Grease Pencil object, target layer and stroke material. For Line Art, set active camera, selected object collection as source, target layer and material. Start with visible contours; add specific creases/material boundaries later.[15] Use a separate layer for hand-animated fixes; Grease Pencil supports per-frame drawings and Dope Sheet/onion skinning.[31] | Avoid outlining twice. Test foreground/background crossings, camera moves, hair across the face and line weight in the final image. Bake/finish only on a copy after camera lock. |

**Exercise:** Render one character with three bands, then two, then without cheek strokes. If eyes and expression fail without interior lines, correct eye shape, gaze and value separation first. *Wing It!* is a production reference for deliberate cartoon imagery, not evidence of a particular node.[28] [Sources](sources.md).

## Character materials: choose what follows the light

**Blender Workshop by Noa3 · 4.5.9 LTS.** Put the same neutral head under a white area light. On a cel face, ask *where should the graphic shadow edge land?* On realistic skin, ask *how wide is the reflected light, and how far does light spread beneath the surface?* One roughness slider cannot answer all three questions. The head graphs are proposed recipes, not proprietary replicas or measured presets. The closed-slab scattering control at the end is a separate executed diagnostic.

### What the projects actually define

- **Koikatsu family ≠ KKBP.** The public material evidence here is from a **community Blender port**, not the proprietary base-game shader. In the pinned V6-era fork, `importcolors.py` chooses `colorsLight` or `colorsDark` for both face and body; that revision even hardcodes a dark skin value. The face exposes separate blush, mouth-interior and lipstick controls; the eyeline has its own base, fade and shadow colors. That is evidence for an artist-directed palette and separate feature treatment in this port, not proof of how every game or mod computes lighting. [400] The KKBP wiki separately documents toon ramps, exporter-created eye texture, and a `Permalight` image whose red and blue channels force light and dark respectively. Do not assume that every version of the wiki and the fork share the same node tree. [401]
- **Daz is not one look.** Iray Uber offers Metallicity/Roughness, Specular/Glossiness and Weighted base mixing; the public Uber skin syllabus distinguishes dual lobe, glossy, top coat and volume scattering. [402][404] Genesis 8.1 instead introduced a PBR Skin shader with changed SSS and support for tiling micro normals, alongside options for lashes and a sculpted iris with matching texture. [403] Even the official bridge is a translation: in the pinned `get_mat_type`, actor `Iray Uber` and `PBRSkin` both route to `IrayUberSkin`, while `Cornea`/moisture route to `EyeWet` and lashes have a separate branch. The shared destination does not imply identical source shaders or render parity. [405]

### A. Build a two-palette face, then decide who owns the shadow

**Input contract.** Paint your own light and shadow colors or maps; do not multiply a finished beauty texture that already has highlights and shadows baked in. Load painted color as **sRGB**. Load thresholds, region IDs, roughness, opacity masks and tangent normals as **Non-Color**; use Separate Color on a packed RGB data image. Use an image’s Alpha output for its embedded alpha. Keep painted UV detail, shadow *decision*, and output color as separate groups so you can inspect them independently.

**EEVEE base graph:** white `Diffuse BSDF → Shader to RGB: Color → RGB to BW → Color Ramp`. Give the ramp a black and a white stop near the chosen terminator; start with closely spaced Linear stops, then compare Constant interpolation at final pixel size. Send the ramp factor to `Mix (Color)` with **A = shadow palette**, **B = lit palette**, then `Emission` strength 1 → `Material Output`. Use a *white* probe BSDF so a dark eyebrow texture does not vote for shadow. Shader to RGB is EEVEE-only; Blender warns about render-pass and reflection/SSS limitations. This palette output is deliberately not a general PBR material. [4]

**Face-specific alternative, portable and deliberately art-directed:** paint a UV grayscale threshold field `T` increasing across the cheek in the order that it should enter shadow; preview the channel as unlit gray first. Define a designated key direction **from face to light**, and transform it into a head-local orthonormal basis (head forward = F, character right = R). Supply the normalized direction via driven Value nodes or external controls; a single fixed world-space vector in the material will not follow a rotating lamp. Project out vertical and normalize horizontally. Compute `front = dot(Lhorizontal,F)`; `amount = (1 - front)/2`. Select the painted field or its mirrored UV copy by the sign of `dot(Lhorizontal,R)` (mirror **within that face island**, or supply a second independently painted map). Compute `lit = smoothstep(amount-w, amount+w, T)` using a clamped **Map Range / Smooth Step**; start `w=0.025`. With this convention low T enters shadow first. Feed `lit` to the palette mix; visualize the factor on a head before coloring. Reserve poles near straight overhead/below for a separate top-light mask or fixed fallback rather than normalizing a nearly zero projection. At dead front/back make both variants converge or blend across a narrow side-selector interval. This is an original threshold recipe, **not** a claim that KKBP uses a signed-distance face map.

**Keep exceptions explicit:** for nonoverlapping artist masks `R` (force lit) and `B` (force shadow), use `max(lit,R) × (1-B)`; this gives shadow priority if the painter overlaps them. For limbs or neck, keep the ordinary diffuse ramp; soften the seam under the jaw. On a Cycles job use the explicit-direction branch, not Shader to RGB; it follows only the nominated key and has no automatic cast shadows. Add occlusion separately if required. Exporting textures does not export this behavior; reimplement the threshold logic in the destination renderer or accept a fixed baked look.

**Executed palette check:** [the original two-palette probe](../scripts/probe_palette.py) renders a UV chart with this head-local threshold in Cycles. Opposite key directions exchanged the light and dark swatches. Turning the head alone reversed the pair; turning head and key together kept it unchanged. A deliberately wrong world-fixed control failed to reverse with the head. This tests the coordinate choice from rendered pixels; it is not an image match to Koikatsu, an EEVEE ramp test or a finished head. For an outline, separate the outer silhouette from drawn lid, nose and mouth lines: use a silhouette line set or an inverted-hull pass for the former, authored curves/UV strokes for the latter. Test a distant head and a close three-quarter view; suppress double contours where the lash already supplies the edge.

**Eye and lash designs:** put the anime iris/pupil and upper iris gradient on an eye surface that aims with the character. Keep graphic glints on a separately controllable layer: turn off painted glints when a moving physical reflection should be the sole catchlight. Place the dark upper lash along the lid edge, not over the pupil on a head-fixed card. For cards use color → Principled Base Color and opacity → Alpha; in EEVEE test the surface render method, overlapping cards and shadows against both white and black. If a halo changes with background, inspect transparent-edge RGB and alpha interpretation before increasing lash thickness. KKBP’s outline fallback (red mask, then main texture alpha) is specific to its outlines, not a universal lash-map convention. [401]

### B. Build a skin response without counting specular twice

1. **Calibrate size and color.** Measure the existing head in scene units, apply intended object scale, lock camera, exposure, view transform and light size. Use de-lit albedo (sRGB) → Principled **Base Color**; metallic 0. Put a gray reference next to the cheek. Map roughness (Non-Color) directly or invert a *verified glossiness* map first, never both. Do not treat a specular intensity map as roughness. Disable clearcoat, painted shine and beauty LUTs for the first comparison.
2. **Scattering before pores.** With Principled, set **Subsurface Weight** above zero, keep red radius longer than green/blue as an initial experiment, and adjust **Scale** against measured head size and a backlit ear. Compare zero weight, a modest weight, then a larger radius at fixed weight: does the terminator soften while nostril, lip and eye-socket contrast survive? Reduce scale before repainting albedo if the entire face turns waxy. Principled places diffuse/SSS under its specular layer; coat is an additional layer, not a synonym for skin scattering. [3]
3. **Separate macro shape and pore scale.** Non-Color tangent normal → `Normal Map` (Tangent, matching UV) → `Bump: Normal`; an independently authored micro-height → `Bump: Height`; output → Principled Normal. Select a small Bump Distance relative to head size; inspect at final resolution. If the source is a second **normal** texture, use a proper tangent-normal blend rather than feeding RGB normals into Height or adding their colors. Check green-channel convention under a moving grazing light; invert the green channel only if the source convention is opposite. Keep pore detail off the lash cards and attenuate it at the wet lip. Do not apply the same pores again as displacement.
4. **Approximate two widths, not two full-strength reflections.** Duplicate the completed Principled skin shader; link identical albedo, SSS, IOR, normal and specular level to both; leave Coat at zero. Give broad branch A roughness 0.48 and tight branch B 0.22 as starting values. `Mix Shader(A,B, factor = 0.20 × oily-region-mask)` → Surface. The weights sum to one, so this changes the distribution of highlights rather than adding a second full skin closure. Start with the broad branch alone; enable the mix under a moving strip light. If the nose becomes varnished, reduce the tight fraction before darkening albedo. Do **not** add a Glossy shader or full-strength coat on top of both. These figures are workshop controls, not Daz preset values or a pin-for-pin MDL conversion.

#### Small surfaces that determine whether the face works

| Surface | Starting construction | Reject this result |
|---|---|---|
| Realistic eye | Off-white sclera with restrained color variation and an iris surface beneath a convex, clear corneal shell. Use a single transmissive dielectric Principled shell (Transmission Weight 1, low roughness; start IOR near 1.38 as an artistic trial). Keep dark pupil separate from corneal highlight; compare against the eye with the shell disabled. | Two unrelated catchlights from duplicated wet shells or a baked highlight; a floating iris at three-quarter view. Inspect inside/outside normals, spacing and engine refraction before editing iris color. |
| Realistic lashes and wet margin | Tapered curves for a hero close-up or masked cards for distant use. Root to the lid; keep the narrow tear margin a separate wet treatment, not white paint. | A black rectangular card shadow, eyelash roots hanging in air, or tear line glowing in an unlit eye. |
| Teeth, gums, tongue | Give enamel a nonmetallic warm off-white base, modest subsurface effect and a controlled smooth highlight; differentiate gum color/roughness, and put wetness only where the mouth exposes it. Anchor upper/lower arches to their respective supports. | A uniformly white luminous bar when lips part. Turn emission off, restore interdental silhouettes and check oral-cavity shadow before yellowing the teeth. |
| Exposed dry bone, not enamel | Principled dielectric, muted ivory base, higher roughness than teeth, restrained broad color variation; use fine irregular height at small amplitude. Reserve stronger cavities for genuinely porous cut surfaces, not every polished cortical surface. | Lacquered skull, black baked cracks under every light, or bathroom-tile gloss shared with teeth. Use the same strip light on adjacent test patches to separate the responses. |

**Proof sheet to make on your own licensed asset:** use the same camera, transform and exposure for front soft light, left and right grazing strip, and backlit ear. With lamp fixed, rotate the head through front, three-quarter and profile; then fix the head and move the lamp. On cel skin inspect mask-only, palette-only, final; the shadow must change cheeks when relative direction reverses without swimming across the nose. On realistic skin compare clay, albedo-only, broad-only, broad+tight, no-SSS and final. If a dark mark rotates with the UV in every light, look for baked shading; if fine dots reverse relief when the key moves, check normals; if eyes turn black only in one engine, isolate corneal transmission and test that engine’s ray/refraction settings. Keep the preferred frame **and the failed comparison** with settings, renderer and scale. No test sheet is claimed here.

**Rights boundary:** public source inspection and a converter are not licenses to redistribute game meshes, commercial character maps, Daz presets, avatar add-ons, video stills or music. Build the graphs with original or appropriately licensed geometry/maps; record separate rights for renders, editable deliverables and engine distribution. No third-party asset is included.

### Measured scattering control, not a complexion preset

[Run the static subsurface probe](../scripts/probe_skin.py) in an empty Blender 4.5 background process. It has neutral albedo, unequal RGB scattering radii and an area source behind closed slabs. In the 2 mm sample the central linear RGB mean was approximately (4.093, 0.609, 0.020). The 12 mm sample returned zero at this sampling budget, as did the thin slab with either the light or the Subsurface Weight disabled. This establishes thickness- and channel-dependent transport in the specified Cycles graph. It does not calibrate human tissue, prove an exact DAZ transfer or imply that every thick sample transmits literally zero light.

## Expressive heads: make the change of thought readable

**Blender Workshop by Noa3 · 4.5.9 LTS.** First action: on an existing head, cover the mouth and ask what the eyes say. Then cover the eyes. If neither part reads, decide which one should carry the thought before adding controls. The head exercises are proposals; the test below measures an original abstract eyelid fixture, not a full acting rig or a new animation.

### Where to look, and what the workshop uses

The linked channel displays as **Hash**. The two public originals checked were *Salt/ソルト – Face Tracking Add-on* and *NyaNyaMaru/にゃにゃまる – Face Tracking Add-on*. They are avatar face-tracking showcases, not Blender rigging tutorials. The watch pages and their timed player previews were inspected, not full-rate playback. They do not establish exact blink timing or the author's rig. [396][397][398]

**Human-viewing references only.** Hash's shop pages credit Komado for Salt and Kai 9 for NyaNyaMaru; the terms on both pages ask that the add-on and promotional material not be used in AI training or AI products. [392][393] Keep only bibliographic links in this workshop. Do not build a training set, frame sequence, motion trace or imitation target from these videos. No images, audio, expression data, title-specific pose sheets, model weights or commercial assets are included. The following methods use independently constructed controls and a synthetic test, not either creator's asset or performance.

For an editable practice character, Blender Studio provides **Rain v2** under CC BY; keep the rig's requested attribution if you use it. The rig page also describes the combination of bendy bones, detail/corrective shapes and Corrective Smooth. [394] Before changing a real rig, test what happens when one existing controller moves: inspect the bone, driven key and last smoothing stage separately instead of adding a second controller to solve the same deformation. The probe below uses no Rain data.

### Build the head that can make the expression

1. **Start with mass, not lips.** On a whole figure, block rib cage, pelvis and shoulder girdle; connect the neck into the trunk before sizing the head. In front, side and three-quarter views mark brow ridge, cheekbone, jaw angle, chin, ear and muzzle/dental projection. Compare with licensed reference at the intended camera distance. A frontal likeness can hide a jaw that collapses into the throat in profile.
2. **Choose what survives simplification.** For an anime avatar, enlarge the eye opening if the design calls for it, but seat an eye surface behind it and wrap both lids around that surface. Give the far cheek and the lip line a readable silhouette in three-quarter view. For hyperreal work, build the orbital rim, lid margins, tear corner, cheek fat, upper-lip bow and chin pad as volumes; reserve pores for after these planes work in gray clay. For a muzzle, block both muzzle pads, nose and lower jaw before cutting a mouth slit. Do not scale a human mouth key as a substitute.
3. **Make the parts meet.** Build concentric deforming loops around mouth and eye openings; redirect loops around the cheek rather than placing a pole at a moving mouth corner. Add inner lip and lid thickness, a recessed mouth bag, teeth/gums and tongue. Use separate eyeballs or a designed eye surface. Test a closed blink and closed-lip silhouette before UVs, detail bakes or hair. Stabilize topology before shape-key production: a Blender key stores positions on the existing mesh, not a new topology. [18]
4. **Tie surface motion to anatomy.** Orbicularis oculi closes the eye, orbicularis oris moves the lips, and the occipitofrontalis moves the brows/scalp; face muscles can move skin rather than a rigid mass. [399] Model the consequences: cheek lift crowds the lower lid; a funnel gathers the lips rather than just scaling their width. Judge these in side light and profile. Preserve underlying volume in realism; exaggerate aperture and line shape first in stylization.

### Controls, combinations and speech

- **Divide ownership.** Give the jaw one rotational control near the jaw joints, eyes individual pivots with a common aim target, upper teeth to the skull and lower teeth to the jaw. Weight the chin and jaw border before adding lip keys. If jaw rotation already opens the mouth, do not put the same downward motion into every vowel. Keep a neutral Basis and name left/right from the character’s perspective.
- **Lids follow the globe.** Separate `Blink.L/R`, upper-lid raise, lower-lid lift and brow inner/outer controls. At extreme up/down gaze, adjust lid follow without tying brows to eye rotation. Inspect halfway through closure for the lid cutting through the cornea; add a mid-blink shape if the straight vertex path takes a shortcut. Keep the lash roots attached. In the wink, protect the other eye’s pupil visibility; allow a smaller reaction on that cheek rather than mirroring the wink.
- **Correct a combination, not a working single control.** Blender relative keys accumulate, and *New Shape from Mix* records the visible mix. [18] For `Smile.L` + `Squint.L`, capture their full key-only mix as a temporary reference, duplicate that shape, sculpt just the unwanted lid fold away, and make the duplicate relative to the uncorrected reference. Keep the reference value zero. Drive the duplicate with `smile_L * squint_L` using normalized controls. This adds only the correction, not a second smile. Check at (0,0), (1,0), (0,1), (1,1) and halfway. For bone-driven jaw correctives, author the residual in the correct deformation space; do not import a posed, armature-deformed head as a Basis-relative offset.
- **Use speech contacts, not alphabet poses.** Mark a recorded line at audible closures and stressed vowels. For M/B/P, seal both lips with mild compression; for F/V, leave the upper incisors against the lower lip; for an open vowel, drop the jaw; for a rounded vowel, bring the corners inward and project the lips. Use the tongue for a visible L contact. Carry a smile in the cheeks while allowing lip seal to win at closures. Mute audio: can you see the contacts? Unmute: if the face chatters, remove an unstressed mouth pose before smoothing everything.
- **Give the thought a hold.** On a timing sheet, mark *notice → decide → respond*. As a proposed 24 fps exercise, hold the neutral read for six frames, change gaze over two, let one mouth corner start two frames later, then hold the answer for eight. Compare to a version where every control starts together. These are workshop choices, **not measured Hash timings**. Lock eye aim to the object of attention during the hold; try a single blink between thoughts, not on every accent. Inspect between poses, not just at the extremes.

**Acceptance:** at final display size, identify the gaze target and dominant expression with hair, hands, audio and shader effects disabled. Re-enable each layer separately. Reject floating teeth, a white slit through closed lips, crossed eyes at ordinary viewing distance, or a smile that forces both brows to rise. Repair the earliest failing layer—form, deformation, timing, then shading—in that order.

**For a VRM 1.0 avatar, test the import, not only Blender.** Map the independent blink, expression and vowel controls in the exporter. The format separates `overrideBlink`, `overrideMouth` and `overrideLookAt`; `none`, `blend` and `block` are different decisions. [395] Designate which control owns lip closure during a smile. Export a disposable head and combine a partial smile with speech and a blink in the receiving application. If the blink works in isolation but disappears with a faint smile, inspect overrides before reworking the mesh. Custom Blender drivers do not automatically become destination logic. This is an export checklist, not a VRChat or VRM compatibility claim for this study.

### What was measured: the two controls work alone but collide together

In [the disposable lid probe](../scripts/probe_expression.py), a smile lifts the lower lid and a squint lowers the upper lid. Both work singly, but in the full combination the center edges of the original ribbons cross by **1.00 mm**. A shape relative to the uncorrected mix, driven only by the product of the two inputs, leaves a **1.00 mm open gap** at the center. Across 25 static combinations the smallest sampled gap was **0.35 mm**. The solo poses and the opposite eye did not move when the correction was added. A separate one-sided wink closed while the other center stayed **10.00 mm** open. Measurements are from Blender's evaluated mesh, without animation, rendering or external assets. They verify control independence and the residual technique, not a universal eye shape, an expressive performance or an exported tracker.

## Hair and skin in motion: roots, shine and superficial veins

**Author: Noa3.** For a close-up, save three views of the same head: a dark silhouette on gray, a large soft key across the hairline, and a narrow moving strip light behind it. Keep exposure and framing fixed. If the profile is wrong, change the groom; if the geometry holds and only the glint looks wrong, change the material. A good skin material cannot repair a sliding vein or a floating part.

### Choose the representation before adding detail

The visible fibre is not a growing chain of muscles. The hair review distinguishes the fibre produced inside the follicle from the keratinized shaft above the scalp, and describes the cuticle as a protective envelope.[481] In the model, put the **attachment and initial direction at the scalp**, the sweep in the guide, and the glint in the shader. Don't model microscopic cuticle scales for an ordinary head shot. Describe the particular person's part, growth direction, curl, length, density changes and styling from front, crown, nape and profile; don't derive a groom from ancestry, sex or a random seed.

| Camera and output | Geometry and texture to author | First failure to look for |
|---|---|---|
| Strand close-up in Cycles | Sparse guides, interpolated fibres, independent clump, taper and colour variation. Define a part and a hairline before flyaways. | At the grazing edge, a forest of equal-radius, equally long strands replaces the designed outline. Fix the primary locks first. |
| Real-time head or mid-distance crowd | Overlapping curved mesh ribbons with a consistent root-to-tip UV axis; bake coverage, flow/tangent and colour separately. Keep the scalp cap beneath the roots. | Thin alpha lines vanish in lower mips; dense layers sort or darken. Check moving close and far views in the receiving engine, not just in Blender. |
| Anime or graphic locks | Closed, tapered mesh locks with varied cross-sections; group the silhouette into a few masses with intentional gaps. Place deformation loops where the lock bends. Drive a narrow highlight with tangent or head-local light and optionally a painted control mask. | A white streak glued permanently to the screen, or a fringe that cuts through the eyelid when the head nods. Rotate the head and key independently. |

Epic's UE 5.5 documentation allows different strand, card or mesh representations per groom LOD and distinguishes texture layouts; its card attributes are a receiver contract, not a universal channel pack.[482] For cards, use subject-specific alpha coverage with colour dilation beyond the edge; keep coverage/flow data non-colour and verify the target's tangent convention. Compare a reference strand lock to the card under rotating light; an identical front silhouette alone is not enough. In Blender 4.5 EEVEE, compare the **Dithered** and **Blended** render methods with crossing cards; do not promise that an alpha-blended stack sorts correctly in every view.[483]

### Build a groom on a head that will move

1. Make a scalp patch that follows the head's shape and deformation. Give it a stable, named, non-overlapping UV map for roots. Name the cap, brows and lashes separately. Mark the part, whorl and temples, along with no-growth regions. Place a few guides and tilt the head *before* generating children. For a hairline shot check that the patch follows changing forehead shape, not only the head bone.
2. Create a **Hair Curves** object on the scalp. In a generation workflow use **Attach Hair Curves to Surface** if imported/free curves need binding, supply the actual surface and UV map, and attach in rest space when a downstream deformation will carry the curves to the pose. Blender's asset has distinct switches for sampling the attachment UV, snapping a root and aligning to the surface normal. Snapping a point onto the rest mesh is not proof of a live binding.[484]
3. Groom large guides along the hair flow. Start straight hair with long readable sweeps; for curls shape the clump centerline, change its pitch and orientation, then add smaller offsets around it. For braids design the crossing bundles before generating fine strands. Separate fringe, crown, temples and nape; don't interpolate across a part or between opposed guide directions. **Interpolate Hair Curves** exposes the guide count, range, density mask, viewport amount and seed. Use a fixed seed to compare one change at a time.[485]
4. For a cowlick or part, give the two guide regions different debug colours before blending them. Check the part in clay; if scalp shows only when turning, track the roots against the posed scalp before increasing density. Guide selection and root attachment are separate controls.[484][485][487]
5. In 4.5 **Deform Curves on Surface** compares original and evaluated scalp geometry; it reads the surface bindings, mesh rest position and each curve's `surface_uv_coordinate`. The manual also warns that smoothing the subdivision UVs can invalidate attachment coordinates and recommends **UV Smooth: None** for this setup.[487] Don't apply the same rig or generator motion to the groom twice. Mask follow-through from fixed roots to free tips; guide a few heavy clumps, not every filament. Sweep the shoulders and ears and test a held stop: pluck the silhouette with large clumps, not a persistent noise field.

**Native check, not a hairstyle.** The original root-follow probe placed three Hair Curves on one UV-mapped triangle. A shape key tipped and lifted the scalp with unchanged object transforms; the expected root and tip positions came independently from the evaluated triangle's barycentrics and normal. Across seven states, including a return to rest, the muted modifier missed a root by **22.05 mm** and a tip by **23.74 mm**. With the modifier enabled, both worst errors were **below 0.00001 mm** in Blender 4.5.9 LTS. This was a synthetic attachment test, not a tolerance for real hair or a dynamics/lighting test. Repeat the visual comparison on your actual head with subdivision, facial shapes and the final scalp UV.

### Make the lighting distinguish fibre, ribbons and solid locks

In Cycles 4.5, **Principled Hair BSDF** is a hair/fur shader; its melanin controls set pigment, tint is applied after pigment, and random colour/roughness varies by strand. It is explicitly **Cycles Only**.[33] Start with a neutral controlled key, one colour mode, fixed roughness and no random variation. Use the same three locks with three different directions. Rotate the strip across all three: streaks should respond to flow, rather than form three identical painted white bands. Add restrained per-strand variation only after that test. For pale hair, a backlit outer rim is not the whole look: Marschner's research overview distinguishes single-fibre scattering from the contribution of multiple scattering in a mass of lighter hair.[488] Compare the inside of the mass with its lit boundary; don't lighten the base texture until a black-clipping view transform or too little fill has been ruled out.

For cards or EEVEE, try a surface shader with a UV-aligned tangent, directional specular response and an independent coverage mask. For mesh locks use a broad reflection band broken by a finer directional mask; keep the highlight out of base colour unless the style *intends* a painted mark. Check a dark and a bright background for edge halos, animation for moiré, and the shadow on the brow for excessive opacity. The recipes here are lighting tests to run on a character, not a claim of visual agreement between Cycles and a game engine.

**Choosing the fibre model.** On real curves in Cycles, start by changing just **Chiang → radial roughness** while holding roughness along the fibre constant. Then switch the model to **Huang** and compare a circular cross-section with an elliptical one using **Aspect Ratio**. Lock pigment, strand radius, camera and light; rotate the lock about its axis. A bandwidth change and a change in where the glint falls are different observations. Neither model supplies the missing orientation on a flat hair card. These are controls to compare, not measured settings for any particular person's hair.[33]

### Skin, vessels and stretch at three distances

OpenStax distinguishes the epidermis from the vascular connective tissue of the dermis; loose connective and fatty tissue lies underneath.[489] Those are anatomical layers, **not** instructions to build three nested refractive meshes. Build the main body contour at real scale; use geometry or a corrective for displaced pads, supporting tissue and major compressed folds. For moderate detail use displacement or normals in surface space, and reserve colour, roughness and very fine bump for smaller changes. Connect albedo in its colour space; read height, normal, masks and roughness as data. Look at the same pose with a neutral clay override and with scattering off. Reuse the [skin transport and eye material checks](character-surfaces.md#chapter-character-shaders) before changing the entire surface.

**A superficial vein on a hand or forearm.** Use a permitted reference of the relevant body area and pose. Draw a few asymmetric, branching curves on the *rest surface*, taper away from the trunk and break or soften visibility at depth; don't stamp an evenly spaced network over the whole body. Bake two masks to the skin UV: a broad soft tint and, only for palpable/raised portions, a weaker, narrower height profile. For a hero hand, instead sculpt the low ridge into the hand's moving surface; a separate round blue hose casts the wrong seam shadow. Keep the deepest branches tint-only. Kienle and colleagues' reflectance work relates perceived vessel colour to the optical properties of tissue, oxygenation, depth, diameter and perception—not actual blue blood.[490] Select hue and contrast under the shot light for the individual's skin. Do not use a fixed bright blue or assume that a change in fullness creates new visible veins.

**Controls and first fixes.** In a flat frontal light, mute just the relief; under a raking light, mute just the colour. If both toggles remove exactly the same thick stripe, decouple the masks. Flex the wrist or change a body state while tracking a curve fork against two skin landmarks; if it swims, replace world-space texturing or rigid parenting with rest UV/bound surface data. On a stretched area, compare a neutral checker and a seam mark: stretching a patch may widen its texture, but it should not grow a new set of disconnected vessels. At full-body distance reduce subpixel contrast before increasing sample count. None of these methods predicts blood flow or diagnoses a person's health.

**Measured A/B.** The vessel-mark probe (historical tool, not included) moves a synthetic patch by 12 pixels in a 96-pixel orthographic Cycles render. Its UV-bound stripe moved **11.999 pixels**; a stripe using world position stayed put. On the neutral diffuse patch, reversing a grazing light reversed the ridge's signed side-to-side contrast (about **+0.398 to −0.398**); the flat control showed no comparable gradient. That answers two separate questions: does the marking stay on the skin, and does its relief actually respond to light? It does not establish the colour, shape or depth of a real vein. Run the probe with a new JSON filename in a separate bpy-enabled Python, then run the mark checker (historical tool, not included) on that file.
